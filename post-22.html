How I Found Holistique: Three Small Miracles, a Gong Bath and a Tribe That Feels Like Home

I was partying, modelling and running my body into the ground. A few needles, one strange afternoon with the gongs and a handful of good people turned my life in a completely different direction.

By Yvonne · Oct 22, 2026 · 8 Min Read

This journal piece is personal writing. It is not medical advice, and it does not promise to treat, cure or relieve any health condition.

I was partying, modelling and running my body into the ground. A few needles, one strange afternoon with the gongs and a handful of good people turned my life in a completely different direction.

If you’d met me a few years ago, I don’t think you’d have guessed I’d end up here, treating people with acupuncture, playing gongs and bringing strangers together who end up as friends. Back then I was modelling, I was out partying a lot, and I treated my body as if it were a machine that would just keep going no matter what I put it through.

Of course, it didn’t keep going. Bodies don’t, not forever, and mine had a way of telling me so that I couldn’t ignore. What surprised me wasn’t that things went wrong. It was what finally helped me put them right, and where that ended up taking me.

Three small miracles

The first sign was my eye. All those late nights caught up with me, and I ended up with a chalazion, which is a little bump on the eyelid. It sounds like nothing, and most of the time it is nothing, but mine simply would not go away. It sat there for months. When your face is your work, a lump on your eyelid isn’t a small thing, and it really did start to affect my modelling jobs. I tried waiting it out, and I kept waiting, and nothing changed.

In the end a friend offered to give me acupuncture and moxa. I didn’t go in with any great expectations; I think I was just tired of looking at it in the mirror every morning. And then, the very next day, it drained. Months of that bump, and it was gone overnight. I remember being quietly amazed, but I filed it away as a lucky one-off.

The second time was at Christmas, which is about the worst time of year to be ill. I’d come down with food poisoning just before a special molecular cuisine dinner, the kind of meal you look forward to for weeks, and I went anyway. It was just soup, but the moment it went down it felt like acid falling into my stomach. Instead of enjoying the festive season, I was curled up feeling wretched.

Then I had acupuncture, and by the next day I could eat again. Not picking at dry toast and hoping for the best, but properly eating. That was the moment the idea of a lucky one-off started to feel a bit thin.

The third time was the hardest, and the one that changed everything. I had just left a relationship that was very stressful, the most difficult one of my life, and I won’t go into it here except to say that my body was carrying all of it. I developed alopecia. Bald patches appeared on my head, round spots about the size of coins, and there is something about finding those that frightens you in a way that’s hard to explain. Your hair feels like part of who you are, and suddenly it’s leaving you.

After one session of acupuncture, my hair started growing back. I still remember running my fingers over those patches and feeling it there, like a soft carpet of baby hair. It took a few years before it reached the same length as the rest, so this wasn’t some overnight transformation, but it came back, and it kept coming back.

Each time, it felt like a miracle.

Three times my body had been in trouble, and three times acupuncture had helped when nothing else had. Each time it felt like a miracle, and after the third I couldn’t just keep calling it luck. I wanted to understand what was happening, and I wanted to be able to do it for other people. So I decided to learn acupuncture myself.

The 4am flight that ended modelling for me

By now I was on a real health journey, trying to look after myself properly for the first time in years, and modelling didn’t sit well with it at all. The hours were all over the place. Early calls, late finishes, days that didn’t follow any kind of rhythm. It’s very hard to rest, eat well and heal when you never know what time you’ll be up tomorrow.

The turning point was a job in Italy. The client had booked flights that meant leaving the house at 4am, and if you’ve ever dragged yourself out of bed at that hour, half asleep and fumbling for your bag in the dark, you’ll know how it makes you feel for the rest of the day. It was terrible for my sleep, which was exactly the thing I was trying to protect. And when we got there, they barely fed us anything. Here I was, working so hard to look after my body, and spending my days hungry and exhausted for the sake of a few photos.

Somewhere in that trip the whole thing started to feel vacuous. I remember thinking, quite clearly, that this wasn’t the life I was trying to build. So I stepped away from modelling and went professional with acupuncture instead, and I’ve never really looked back.

The gong bath where I saw dinosaurs

Then there were the gongs, which came into my life in the strangest and loveliest way.

My first gong bath, I lay down not knowing quite what to expect. As the sound built and washed over me, I zoned out completely. I was more relaxed than I’d been in a very long time, somewhere between awake and asleep, just floating in the sound. And then, partway through, I saw dinosaurs. I know how that sounds. But it really was like time travelling, as if the gongs had carried me all the way back to their time and let me look around for a while. When it ended I just lay there, a bit stunned, feeling that something magical had happened.

I’ve loved gongs ever since that afternoon. They give me so much clarity in my life. When I play them, or when I’m lying there listening to someone else play, I get messages, loud and clear. Questions I’ve been going round and round on suddenly have answers, and things I’ve been worrying about settle into place. It’s hard to put into words, which is probably why I keep inviting people to come and feel it for themselves.

Starting from scratch in London

I’ve always created communities around me, wherever I’ve lived. It’s just something I do. But moving to London during the pandemic was tough, and I think a lot of people who moved cities around then will know what I mean. Everything was closed or half closed, everyone was keeping their distance, and it was very hard to meet anyone new.

The people I did know in London mostly came through modelling, and they were, almost all of them, party people. Lovely in their own way, but that was the life I’d just stepped out of, and I didn’t want to slide back into it. I wanted to be around people who cared about the same things I’d started to care about.

So I started from scratch. I began organising holistic events, partly as a way to share the things that had helped me, and partly, if I’m honest, as a way to bring a different kind of person into my life. That was the beginning of Holistique.

The people I found

It worked better than I ever could have hoped. I’ve made so many friends through my events, and my best friends, the people I’d call first about anything, came from them. We’ve travelled together since, and some of them now facilitate Holistique events with me, which still makes me smile when I think about how it all started, with me just wanting a few people to talk to.

I’ve watched other people find each other too. One couple met at my brunch party, moved in together two weeks later, which I remember thinking was either very brave or a little bit mad, and they’re still married now, years on. Every time I think of them I’m reminded that you never really know what will happen when you put the right people in a room together.

Where I treat now

These days I treat people through house visits, and in my peaceful canal-side flat in East London. It’s quiet there, with the water just outside, and it feels a world away from 4am airport runs and soup that tasted like acid. It feels like the right place to help other people with the kinds of things acupuncture once helped me with.

Come home

When someone walks into a Holistique event for the first time, maybe a little nervous, maybe not quite sure what they’ve signed up for, I hope they feel like they’ve arrived home. That’s really all I’ve ever wanted from it. Somewhere you can relax, meet good people and leave feeling a bit lighter than when you came in.
