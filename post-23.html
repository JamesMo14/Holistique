A Slower Cup: Tea, Buckwheat and the Kitchen Habits That Keep Me Steady

Simple swaps for a calmer kitchen this winter, from buckwheat bowls to good olive oil, plus two chances to try a Chinese tea ceremony in London.

Pouring tea at a Holistique Chinese tea ceremony. Photo: Holistique UK

Some of the calmest moments in a day happen in the kitchen, not on a mat. Putting the kettle on, stirring a pot, pouring tea slowly instead of gulping it at my desk.

So this one is about food and drink: a few things I use myself, and one ritual I’d love more people to try.

Start with the tea

A Chinese tea ceremony is one of the easiest ways I know to slow down. It’s called Gongfu Cha, which means “making tea with skill”. You brew, pour and taste small cups of fine tea, and hear a little about where the ritual comes from. No experience needed. You just sit, sip and pay attention.

There are two chances to try it with us this month:

  • Chinese Tea Ceremony Workshop, Sat 14 Nov, 2:30pm to 5:30pm, Social Yoga Fish Island, east London. Book on Eventbrite
  • Holistic Market & Chinese Tea Ceremony, Fri 20 Nov, 6pm to 9pm, The Amadeus, Little Venice. The tea space becomes a market with holistic vendors and astrology readings. You can pre-book the guided ceremony for £25, or get a £5 market pass that counts towards the ceremony on the night. Made with Qi Tea House. Book on Dandelion

Keep it simple

You don’t need all of this. If I had to pick two, I’d start with good olive oil and a warm bowl of buckwheat, because you’ll taste the difference this week. Then add one drink if it helps.

And next time you put the kettle on, try pouring slowly and actually watching it. That’s the tea ceremony in miniature.
