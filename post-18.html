Paws & Sound: What to Expect at London’s Doggy Sound Healing Experience

What to bring

By Yvonne · Sep 29, 2026 · 5 Min Read

If Day 1 made you curious, this is the practical piece.

Paws & Sound: Doggy Sound Healing Experience is on Saturday 10 October 2026 at 7:00pm in London, presented by HolistiqueUK. The short description on the site says it best: chill with your pups and vibe to soothing sounds for a pawsome healing session together.

Below is what that actually feels like to attend — without fluffy mystery, and without turning your dog into a prop.

Who it’s for

This evening suits you if:

  • You want a shared calm experience with your dog, not another high-energy social.
  • You’re open to sound healing (gongs, bowls, soft instrumental texture) even if you’ve never tried it.
  • Your dog can settle in a new indoor space with other calm dogs nearby — or you’re willing to step out if they can’t.

It may not be ideal if your dog is in a highly reactive phase around unfamiliar dogs or loud novel sounds, or if you need a guaranteed silent room. Sound healing is gentle, but it is still sound. Honest assessment beats forced bravery.

The vibe (in plain English)

Expect a held, welcoming room rather than a nightclub wellness mash-up. People arrive with leads, water, and a bit of nerves — that’s normal. Facilitation guides the group into lying or sitting, dogs finding their spot, and then the sound journey unfolds.

Some dogs nap. Some listen with bright eyes. Some need a reassuring hand on the ribs. Humans tend to do the same, only with more overthinking.

The point isn’t perfection. The point is shared rest.

You don’t have to “get it”. You only have to show up and let the room be kind.

Rough shape of the evening

Exact run-of-show can flex on the night, but most dog-friendly sound sessions follow a humane arc:

  1. Arrival & settling — find a mat, toilet your dog if needed, shake off the street.
  2. Short welcome — how the sound will work, house notes, consent to leave if anyone (two-legged or four) needs air.
  3. The sound journey — instruments create a continuous or gently evolving soundscape.
  4. Soft landing — silence, stretch, water, a moment to reconnect before packing up.
  5. Goodbyes — unhurried; nobody is rushed out mid-blink.

Doors and precise venue notes will be on your ticket confirmation via the events hub (https://holistiqueuk.com/events). Arrive with enough time that you’re not transmitting rush into your dog’s leash.

What to bring

A simple kit goes a long way:

  • Lead and waste bags (non-negotiable in London).
  • Water for you and your dog.
  • A familiar mat, towel, or blanket with home scent.
  • A quiet chew or lick mat only if the facilitator says it’s welcome — some rooms prefer minimal extra noise.
  • Layers for you — resting bodies cool down.

Leave the squeaky toys at home. This isn’t a playdate with a soundtrack.

How to help your dog enjoy it

  • Walk beforehand, but don’t exhaust them into wired fatigue.
  • Practise a few minutes of settled mat-time at home in the week before.
  • Sit where your dog can see the room without being in a doorway traffic jam.
  • If they stand and shake off, that’s information — not failure. Step out, reset, return if it feels right.

Your calm is contagious. So is your tension. Breathe on purpose.

Etiquette that keeps the room kind

Dog events live or die on manners — human manners first.

Keep leads manageable so you’re not knitting a web across the aisle. Ask before your dog approaches another dog, even if yours is “friendly”. Wipe paws if the street was wet. If your dog is in a vocal phase, sit nearer an edge so you can step out without a parade.

Photograph sparingly. A quick snap of your own pup is fine; a spotlight on someone else’s animal mid-settle is not. This is a healing room that happens to welcome dogs, not a content set.

Facilitators will set any house rules on the night. When in doubt, choose the option that creates more calm for the whole floor.

If you’re coming solo with a sensitive dog, message any pre-event contact listed on your ticket with a brief note. Teams would rather know than be surprised. Holistique’s tone is welcoming precisely because people communicate early.

Why HolistiqueUK put this on the calendar

HolistiqueUK curates wellness experiences that blend ancient-feeling practices with modern city life — sound healing, meditation, community gatherings, and one-to-one care like acupuncture.

Paws & Sound is the playful cousin of the deeper overnight journeys and day retreats also landing this autumn. Same heart: connection, rest, less performance.

If you love the idea of healing that includes the animal who already knows your nervous system better than most humans, this night was made with you in mind.

Aftercare for dogs (and humans)

When you get home, keep the vibe low. A short garden wee, water, and a familiar bed beat a bright living-room reunion party. Some dogs sleep harder than usual; others want a quiet cuddle and nothing more.

Humans benefit from the same rule. Don’t schedule a loud birthday dinner straight after. Let the Saturday night be a Saturday night. If you notice your dog more settled the next morning — or yourself less braced on the lead — that’s useful feedback for whether to book the next dog-friendly date Holistique offers.
